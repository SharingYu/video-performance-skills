# Original exercise: two library coworkers

**Fictional illustrative text; no media test.** Alex and Robin are invented adult coworkers. No real workplace, people, footage, or model run is represented.

Design a short, warm visual comedy at a public library's display table. It should be understandable through action and reaction, without explanatory captions or spoken explanation. Duration, shot count, and format are open; use only the time the event earns.

## Characters and task

- **Alex:** a new adult staff member who wants to appear efficient and capable. Under pressure, Alex polishes the visible result before checking the small practical detail that makes it work.
- **Robin:** an experienced adult colleague, calm and helpful. Robin wants the display finished and lets Alex correct a mistake without turning it into a public embarrassment.
- **Shared task:** prepare a small book display before visitors arrive. Use ordinary lightweight display copies, stands, and tabletop materials.

## Constraints

- Both coworkers have a purpose and make consequential choices. Robin is not merely a spectator waiting for a punchline.
- Offer three mechanisms that differ in audience expectation, character tactic, and visible consequence; changing only the prop does not count.
- Choose one, explain the editorial choice, and write its six-group event card before proposing a shot list.
- A neutral face or still body may carry a reaction through a change of attention, a stopped action, or a practical response. Do not require a grin, blink, or eyebrow movement at every beat.
- Keep the relationship kind. Avoid a fall, damaged books, danger, a malicious prank, or humiliation as the reward.
- State what the audience knows and when Alex knows it. Protect any required information order rather than adding a surprise by hiding all useful evidence.
- Do not claim that a paper design is funny to an audience, easy for a particular model, or already validated.

You may introduce a modest practical obstacle. Mark invented mechanics and staging as design choices, with anything that needs a later physical or media test left explicit.
