# Worked original example: the display is not quite finished

**Fictional illustrative text; no media test.** This is an authored design example, not an actual agent execution, independent review, audience study, or rendered result. Alex and Robin are fictional adult coworkers.

## Three different mechanisms

| Candidate | Goal, tactic, and audience expectation | Visible deviation and consequence | Main design risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A · Beautiful spacing, wrong measure | Alex uses a ruler to make empty stands perfectly even. The viewer expects a polished display. | The first wide book overlaps the next stand. Robin offers the actual book as the spacing guide; Alex sets the ruler aside and re-spaces from the objects. | Can read as a practical lesson with a predictable correction rather than a character moment. |
| B · Too considerate to finish | At a narrow display opening, each coworker yields to let the other place an item first. The viewer expects cooperation to clear the bottleneck. | Their simultaneous retreats recreate it. Robin stops yielding and offers Alex both items; Alex places them together while Robin opens the next space. | Repetition can look contrived unless the competing intentions and practical exit are clear. |
| C · Finished, except for the helping hand | Alex perfects a display book's upper alignment. The audience can see Robin supporting its lower edge because the stand's small lower ledge is still folded flat. | Alex releases the book and briefly assumes the job is done. Robin naturally offers the next book while continuing to support the first. Alex follows the hand relationship, registers the missing support, opens it, and completes the job for real. | The support, next book, and discovery must all be legible without concealing the only causal clue. |

**Illustrative editorial selection: C.** It links Alex's concern for appearance to a specific oversight and gives Robin useful work throughout. The audience knows about the support before Alex recognizes its significance, so Alex's interrupted “done” posture can be the turn. Correction restores cooperation and dignity. This is an editorial hypothesis, not measured audience preference. A and B remain viable alternatives, not tested failures.

## Event ORIG-01 · Finished, except for the helping hand

Mode: original · Characters: Alex and Robin, adults · Scope: one tabletop event, potentially across several shots.

### 1. Audience expectation and payoff

Show the lower ledge folded flat and Robin's supporting hand early enough for the viewer to understand the problem. The viewer waits for Alex to discover that the apparent finish still depends on Robin.

Alex's release and brief finished posture temporarily assert success. Robin's offer of the next book creates a practical reason for Alex to look along the hands and register what is happening. Alex's reach stops and changes purpose: open the support, then accept the next book. The payoff is a book that stays up after Robin removes the hand, with work resuming rather than anyone laughing at Alex.

### 2. Visible performance chain

| Phase | Trigger and attention | Designed action | Purpose or interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ORIG-01-P1 | Alex attends to the display book's upper edge. Robin attends to keeping that book supported while holding the next copy ready. | Robin supports the lower edge; the small ledge remains folded flat and visible to the audience. Alex adjusts the top. | Establish an imbalance between a polished appearance and unfinished practical support. |
| ORIG-01-P2 | Alex finishes the top adjustment. | Alex releases both hands and settles into a brief finished posture. Robin's support remains. | Alex assumes completion; this is designed intention, not a claim about recorded thought. |
| ORIG-01-P3 | Robin offers the next book without withdrawing the supporting hand. | Alex starts to accept, looks from the offered book toward the supporting hand and the folded ledge, and stops the accepting reach. | The interruption makes recognition visible without needing an exaggerated face. |
| ORIG-01-P4 | Alex's attention is now on the missing support. | Alex redirects the hand to open the ledge beneath the display book while Robin continues supporting it. Robin keeps the next book ready and reduces other movement. | A changed action answers the discovery; Alex performs the correction. |
| ORIG-01-P5 | The book rests on the opened ledge. | Only then does Robin withdraw the supporting hand. The book stays in place. Alex accepts the next book, and both resume the shared task. | Confirm the cause of stability and end on restored cooperation. |

No fixed pause is assigned. Keep only enough finished posture, recognition, and support withdrawal for their changes to read. These phases are not a mandatory sequence of cuts.

### 3. Protected details

| ID | Observable requirement | Order or relationship | Loss if omitted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ORIG-01-A | The folded ledge and Robin's support are visible before Alex's discovery. | The audience knows the practical problem first. | The viewer cannot anticipate the mismatch, or the correction seems arbitrary. |
| ORIG-01-B | Alex genuinely releases the top adjustment and adopts a brief finished posture. | Before the next-book offer interrupts it. | No mistaken completion exists to be revised. |
| ORIG-01-C | The accepting reach stops after Alex attends to the supporting hand/ledge, then changes to opening it. | Offer → attention → interruption → new action. | An automatic repair replaces the character's recognition and choice. |
| ORIG-01-D | Robin supports the book throughout the correction and is also trying to advance the work. | No premature withdrawal or staged wait for embarrassment. | Robin becomes a prankster or an inert reaction prop. |
| ORIG-01-E | The book remains stable after the ledge opens and Robin withdraws the hand. | Open support → transfer load → withdraw → stable result. | The practical consequence, and therefore the payoff, is missing. |

An optional tail could show Alex checking the next stand before placing a book. Keep it only if it adds a readable changed habit rather than repeating the solution. A smile is optional; the stopped reach, changed task, and cooperative continuation already carry the intended response.

### 4. Attention handoffs

- **Alex's precise adjustment → Robin's supporting hand:** establish both in a common spatial relationship. Avoid a crop that makes the audience invent how the book stays up.
- **Robin's offer → Alex's interrupted reach:** the offered book invites action. Robin remains physically useful but avoids an added flourish while Alex registers the support.
- **Alex's correction → the stable book:** keep the support transfer visible. The still book after hand withdrawal is the result to read, not empty waiting.

Alex's gaze target, head movement, and hand target need separate directions in a shot brief. “Alex turns toward Robin” alone does not specify whether Alex sees a face, an offered book, or the supporting hand. Robin can retain a composed expression while responding through the offer, continued support, and timely withdrawal.

### 5. Allowed changes and essential relationships

The room, display theme, clothing, book covers, rendering style, camera angle, and table decoration can change. Use a clear two-person tabletop view as a starting option, adding a detail view only if it helps the same causal geography.

Preserve the division of attention, audience-before-Alex knowledge, practical support, Alex's interrupted tactic, and the stable result. Do not show Alex knowingly checking or opening the ledge before the discovery. Do not hide Robin's support until a surprise insert: this design uses an audience knowledge advantage, not a withheld-cause reveal.

The chosen stand must permit a safe hand-supported book and an accessible hinged lower ledge. If those mechanics do not work in a physical or generated test, redesign the prop while preserving the functional chain; do not silently count a different action as the same payoff.

### 6. Original intent and open tests

- **Original design intent:** affection through a difference in working habits; apparent completion becomes real completion through noticing and helping.
- **Authored assumptions:** Alex's upper-edge attention makes the oversight plausible; Robin's simultaneous support and offer can be staged clearly; the support transfer is readable.
- **Not yet established:** prop mechanics, acting clarity, exact rhythm, media-model feasibility, sound, or whether an audience finds the event amusing.
- **Next design review:** can a reader identify the two goals, the knowledge gap, the changed action, and the consequence without a caption? That is a paper check, not a media pass.
- **Later media review:** check that recognition and redirection are visible between the boundary states. Reject a version that merely begins with adjustment and ends with a stable display while losing the intervening choice.

The deliverable is an event design, ready for a separate shot-planning step.
