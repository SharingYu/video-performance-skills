# Worked reference example: a reach becomes a cover activity

**Fictional illustrative text; no media test.** This is an authored example based only on the seven written records in [input.md](input.md). It is not an actual agent execution, a rendered comparison, or a report of successful performance transfer.

Event: **REF-01** · Mode: reference · Characters: Miso and Nori, anthropomorphic cats · Evidence scope: O1–O7 only.

## 1. Audience expectation and payoff

At first, the viewer can expect Miso to take a snack. The visible gaze change and interrupted reach permit a new reading: Miso has registered Nori's attention. The same paw becomes conspicuously helpful at a placemat that needed no work.

Nori's restrained inspection and later dish movement answer that cover activity without a broad facial reaction. Miso's small follow-and-stop lets the original interest in the food remain visible. “Caught pretending to tidy” is an interpretation of this chain, not an observation of either character's mind.

## 4. Attention handoffs

- **Miso's approach → the meeting of attention:** the gaze change and suspended paw redirect interest to the relationship. Keep both relevant faces and the paw readable, together or with a clearly connected view.
- **Miso's mat work → Nori's inspection:** Miso settles rather than adding a second flourish. Nori's gaze sequence carries the response despite a composed face.
- **Nori's dish movement → Miso's follow-and-stop:** the moving dish is the cue; preserve the responding eyes and head long enough to read the consequence.

Do not conceal the paw at the target change or let unrelated background movement take over the payoff.

## 5. Allowed changes and essential relationships

Table shape, room, light, decoration, and camera position can change. A different harmless snack or tidyable surface is possible if the new design still makes the original target and unnecessary cover activity clear.

Preserve reachability, the same-paw redirection, readable attention, the order of partner response, and the final food-directed response. Do not reveal a planned tidying intention in the opening pose. Do not move the dish into its final position before Nori performs the consequential action.

The viewer need not inspect a matching background. The viewer does need enough scale and an unobstructed view to distinguish “paw stops” from “paw keeps reaching.”

## Same endpoints, different middle: text-only comparison

Both variants start with Miso reaching toward food and end with Miso seated, paws withdrawn, dish nearer Nori. Their boundary states alone cannot distinguish them.

**Degraded design:** Miso extends a paw, then smoothly wipes the mat while looking down throughout. Nori keeps looking elsewhere. Cut to the dish already nearer Nori and Miso seated. The reach and final arrangement exist, but the recognition, interrupted choice, partner's answer, and caused follow-and-stop do not.

**Preserved design:** Miso's food-directed reach stops as Miso looks to Nori; Nori's eyes are on the paw, then their gaze meets. The suspended paw changes course to the already-flat corner. After Miso retracts, Nori inspects corner and cat, then slides the dish closer. Miso tracks that movement, begins to follow with the head, and stops. The composed face has a consequential response rather than decorative motion.

These are contrasting written designs, not generated outputs. The second preserves the intended chain on paper; only actual media review could establish whether viewers can see it.
