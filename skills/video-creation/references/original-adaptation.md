# Original Stories and Reference-Derived Adaptations

Make the story interesting before making its shots elaborate. Begin with a character pursuing something through a particular tactic, a condition that resists it, a choice, and a consequence that matters to that character.

## Choose the input mode

- **Original:** develop from the user's premise, characters, audience promise or theme. No reference search is required unless it would help the requested work.
- **Reference-derived:** inspect the supplied video or other accessible source, distinguish observed material from interpretation, and identify the mechanism the user wants to learn from. Use [reference intake](reference-remake.md) for access and evidence, then return here for a new story.
- **Current-trend request:** establish dated sources or current platform evidence before calling something presently popular. An old example, a repository's marketing or the assistant's memory does not establish current reach. If no current evidence is available, label the trend premise unverified and develop a useful concept without claiming a trend result.

Follow a user-selected idea directly. A local repair does not need three replacement stories. For open concept development, compare a small set of mechanisms that actually differ in character tactic, pressure, information or consequence. Changing the food, costume, job or background alone is not a different dramatic mechanism.

## Develop the audience-facing mechanism

For each useful candidate, state:

| Question | Make it concrete |
|---|---|
| What does the character want now? | A task or result that can drive a visible choice |
| What method would this character try? | A habit, strategy or value under pressure |
| What makes it difficult? | An established condition, partner strategy or cost |
| What does the viewer expect? | A clear result, discovery or emotional promise |
| Which choice changes the situation? | An action caused by the character and current conditions |
| What lands? | A result, reaction, contrast or changed relationship the viewer can read |

A new random obstacle can be surprising without being a satisfying payoff. A hook creates a worthwhile expectation; later actions must develop or answer it. Warmth, relief, recognition, process satisfaction and suspense are valid rewards alongside comedy.

In a reference-derived adaptation, record the learned mechanism and the meaningful changes to goal, strategy, interaction or outcome. If only the cast or props changed while the same story was rebuilt, label it as that exercise rather than presenting it as an independently developed story.

## Select and make it playable

Assess the candidates for readable cause and effect, character specificity, payoff, and production burden. If useful independent review is available, give the brief and candidates without a preferred answer. Otherwise label self-review honestly. Do not ask the user to approve every ordinary shot or treat repeated assistant agreement as audience evidence.

Useful diagnoses include an answer revealed too early, missing context, excessive explanation, an out-of-character tactic, and setup that no longer changes an expectation. Repair the relevant cause instead of adding a universal quota of twists, close-ups or micro-expressions.

Write the chosen story as a chain of visible changes: starting situation, pursuit, tactic under pressure, consequential choice and result. Give both members of an ensemble a purpose when the story relies on their relationship. A reserved character can act through attention, restraint or a practical decision; an energetic one need not always be foolish.

For a key highlight, make a compact performance card covering viewer expectation/payoff, visible action chain, protected details, attention handoff, allowed changes and original intent. With `performance-direction` installed, use it to deepen the card. Specify what is actually visible: a stopped reach, a body that follows and catches itself, an unequal effort, a partner's useful response. A label such as "cute" or "awkward" is not enough direction.

## Move into shots and production

Assign shots to story and performance duties, not to a generic far/medium/close sequence. Choose a view where the relevant action, target and response can be read. Decide what the opening image should reveal or withhold. A static two-character view may carry an interaction better than cutting every phase apart.

Use [shared handoff](shared-handoff.md) for assets, backend-ready shot briefs, selected ranges and review. Let useful action determine the edit; separate generation length from selected length and remove repeated waiting while keeping meaningful anticipation and aftermath.

Deliver the candidate comparison when relevant, selected causal story, performance highlights and requested downstream work. Proposed comic or emotional effect is a creative hypothesis until actual media and audience evidence exist. No workflow, trend label or skill collection guarantees attention or commercial performance.
