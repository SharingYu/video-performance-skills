# Recreate a Linked or Local Reference

The job is to preserve the user's chosen story or viewing effect while rebuilding the permitted elements. Establish what the source actually shows before adapting it. A fluent summary can still have the wrong action, prop or interaction.

## Establish access and coverage

Record the supplied URL or local file, source identity, available metadata, and the material actually accessed. A page description, title, comments or transcript can support their own content; none establishes that the assistant watched the video.

Use the host's supported connector, platform reader or local-media workflow. Existing authorized sessions may be reused. Do not assume that a text-reading tool can retrieve playable media, or that every link is downloadable. When access is unavailable, retain the source as pending, state the missing material, and continue cast/style preparation or a provisional intake plan that does not invent the source story.

Once actual media is available, record its version or hash, duration/time origin, and viewed ranges. If `video-observation` is installed, use its overview, window, crop and observer-note tools. Otherwise use available host tools to obtain ordered frames with source locations. Actually open returned images; reopen images discarded by a truncated response. Static images establish only the visible poses and sequence they contain; real-time timing and audio need their own observations.

## Read the story through action

Cover the shots before selecting only the moments already assumed to be important. For a useful moment, record:

| Source location | Directly visible action and broad attention | Partner response / result | Interpretation and uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Actual source version and interval or frames | Who approaches, handles, turns, stops or withdraws | What happens because of it | Intended meaning is separate from evidence |

Use normal audience-visible granularity unless the user asks for closer analysis. Approximate head/body direction and a readable attention target are useful; do not invent exact eye direction from handling an object. A quiet performer may still be following, assessing or responding. Revisit an uncertain action with context before and after it, keeping the other performer and object available in the full view.

Distinguish an action shown from an outcome revealed after an omission or cut. Do not add an unseen transaction, off-screen punishment, spoken line or motivation merely because it explains the plot.

## Protect the appeal before replacing the shell

Identify the few moments that change the viewer's expectation or reward it. Preserve the trigger, action turn, reaction, information order and aftermath that carry the effect. Appearance matching and a completed physical task are not sufficient tests.

For each protected event, make a compact card:

1. What the viewer expects and what lands instead or afterward.
2. The visible chain of attention, choice, action and response.
3. Stable protected-detail IDs with observable requirements and order.
4. Who holds attention, and the cue that hands it to another performer.
5. What can change, and which functional relationships must remain.
6. Source evidence, directing interpretation and unknowns.

Use `performance-direction` if installed for deeper event-card work. Scenery can change when allowed, while reachability, concealment, prop function, line of sight or relative timing may be essential. Matching opening and ending images cannot prove a meaningful middle performance survived.

## Rebuild and hand off

Assign the new cast according to their established traits and goals. Preserve identity facts the user locked without forcing the old actor's exact facial mechanics onto another species or style. State substantive adaptation changes and why they preserve the intended effect. Do not replace a requested same-story exercise with an unrelated story.

Write the reconstruction as concrete action and reciprocal consequences, then give each shot a responsibility, starting state, meaningful change and cutting opportunity. One acting phase is not automatically one cut. Plan a simpler alternative for a hard interaction while retaining its protected function.

Continue through [shared handoff](shared-handoff.md) to assets, keyframes, the supported backend, selection and review when requested and authorized. Separate requested generation duration from the useful edit. If a protected reaction is absent or unreadable, repair its staging, source input, performance or selection rather than changing the shot's duty after failure.

Deliver the source/coverage record, causal reconstruction, protected events, permitted changes and the requested downstream artifacts. Keep unobserved rhythm, audio and obscured action explicitly pending.
