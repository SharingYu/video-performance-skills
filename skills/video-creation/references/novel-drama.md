# Adapt a Novel into a Comic Drama or Serial

Treat the novel as source material with characters, causes and promises, rather than as prose to paraphrase over images. A chapter boundary is not an episode boundary. A short excerpt supports development from that excerpt, not a claim to know the entire book.

## Intake and production form

The basic text intake is a local UTF-8 TXT or Markdown source. Keep the original intact and record its version/hash. If the user supplies EPUB, PDF, scans or an online source, use an available extraction tool and verify a sample before treating the extracted text as reliable. This skill does not bundle those converters or access to a paid novel service.

Preserve the user's adaptation scope and locked character, relationship, world-rule or ending facts. Separate a user-supplied ending from an ending actually read in the novel. Record relevant source and intended-use information without treating possession of a file as proof of commercial publication clearance.

For a Hongguo-oriented project, determine whether the intended production is narration with panels/limited movement, character-dialogue animation, or a mixture. These forms affect cast voice consistency, lip sync, performance, shot complexity and editing. If the form is unresolved, compare the meaningful tradeoff while continuing text analysis. Do not invent a platform-mandated episode count, duration, paywall or hook formula.

## Read in recoverable units

Use chapter headings and file boundaries as candidate navigation, then inspect the actual text to understand scenes, viewpoints, time jumps and causal units. Choose a manageable reading batch from the source size and current task. Keep exact source locators and read coverage; an index or regex match is not semantic reading.

Maintain a compact source ledger:

- **Facts and causes:** what happens, what changes, and which source passage supports it.
- **People and aliases:** stable character IDs, witnessed names and relationships, with unresolved alias candidates kept provisional.
- **Knowledge and rules:** who knows what at this point; the conditions and costs of established world rules.
- **Setups and consequences:** what is planted, what later evidence answers it, and what remains unresolved or unread.
- **Source uncertainty:** contradictory accounts, unreliable viewpoints, omissions and material not yet read.

Summaries should carry the function of a passage and its evidence location, not replace access to the source. Do not bring unread future knowledge into a character's current choices. On resume, verify the current source version, completed coverage, accepted canon and the next pending unit. A changed source invalidates affected locators and derivative claims; it is not silently merged into old records.

For continuing work, use or adapt the [series ledger](../assets/series-ledger.md). Keep aliases provisional until the source supports a link, and distinguish reader knowledge, narrator claims and each character's knowledge. The ledger is not required for a self-contained one-scene exercise.

The optional [project helper](../scripts/project_tool.py) can preserve intake and progress records. It does not infer aliases, understand chapters, identify payoffs or produce an adaptation. An existing script that indexes already-numbered episodes must not be presented as semantic novel adaptation.

## Find the serial engine and adaptation choices

Identify the audience's recurring emotional or dramatic reward, the protagonist's immediate pursuit and habitual tactic, the force that resists it, and how choices change stakes or relationships. A serial needs a way to develop pressure and a meaningful stopping condition, not the same argument with new insults.

Separate:

| Category | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Locked promises | Preserve agreed character positions, core relations, causal rules and ending meaning |
| Replaceable carriers | Compress, reorder or change how an event becomes visible while preserving its function |
| Redundancy | Remove repetition that creates no new choice, knowledge, cost or consequence |
| New design | Mark additions, merges, moved reveals and new dialogue as adaptation choices, not source facts |

Keep a short adaptation map from source unit/locator to its dramatic function and destination episode/scene. When a later high point is moved forward, carry the setup and knowledge conditions it needs. Avoid claiming that a dramatic rule came from the platform when it is only a selected craft approach.

## Build episodes around changed situations

Group source material by playable goals, resistance, decisions and consequences. Each episode should establish a worthwhile expectation, develop it through action and give a readable local result. Its ending can leave a specific danger, decision, unanswered question or emotional pressure that the next episode genuinely inherits.

Record the entering state, pursuit, change, local result, information released, outgoing pressure and next inherited state. Placeholders for unread material remain provisional. Do not lock a whole season's exact episode count from an opening excerpt.

Start with a small requested or context-appropriate writing batch that can test the engine and neighboring-episode handoff. Write scenes in visible actions and purposeful dialogue. Convert internal prose into a playable choice, interaction, evidence or deliberate narration; narration need not restate what is already shown. Use existing development/writing/storyboard modules only when available and useful.

Review whether the audience understands the pursuit, what changes, what they receive now and why they would continue. Compare adjacent episode knowledge, props, relationships, injuries, unresolved setups and promised consequences. Do not force every genre into a humiliation/revenge victory or a fixed interval of reversals.

## Handoff and readiness

Pass the accepted sample and source map into [shared handoff](shared-handoff.md): recurring assets and variants, per-episode states, shot duties, performance events, voice/dialogue requirements and actual backend work. A narrator-led pilot does not validate a multi-character dialogue pipeline; a successful independent clip does not validate a whole season.

Report readiness separately: text intake and coverage, adaptation development, episode/script quality, visual consistency, voices/dialogue/lip sync when needed, edited media, and publication conditions. Current platform specifications, account/cooperation access and source-use conditions require current evidence before claiming publication readiness. Method availability or a package's sales claim cannot establish Hongguo acceptance or a viral result.

Deliver the brief, read-scope/source ledger, adaptation decisions, episode map, current script batch and continuity handoff at the requested stage. Leave untested media capabilities explicit while continuing the stages that are available.
