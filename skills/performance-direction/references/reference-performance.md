# Reference Performance

Adapt what makes a reference readable and engaging. Do not assume the task is pixel matching or plot summarization.

## Find the moments that matter

Read the brief for protected facts and permitted changes. Inspect the context around the important action, then examine its preparation, peak, reaction, and aftermath. Use closer temporal inspection for brief details when the host supports it. Keep a record of what was actually viewed and heard.

For each candidate moment, ask:

- What does the performer notice, and what visible evidence establishes that? Keep body facing, head facing, eye direction, and manipulation target separate.
- What is the immediate task or apparent strategy? Label inferred intentions; observable actions are the evidence.
- Which interruption, weight shift, hesitation, contact, overreach, reaction, or recovery gives the action character?
- Who registers the consequence? Does a second character's restraint, delayed recognition, or contrasting response finish the moment?
- Can the audience notice the important detail in the full composition, or only in an enlarged analytical crop?

Select the few details that determine meaning or appeal. Do not require every incidental blink, strand of hair, or ornament to be copied. Use [event cards](event-card.md) to identify the protected details and their evidence.

## Separate expression from scenery

Changing a background can be acceptable while changing the relationship that creates the moment is not. A prop's material may be replaceable; its function, size, weight, or visibility may be essential. A new angle may work if it preserves the reveal order and lets the action and response be read.

Before accepting an alternative, state what the audience must still understand or experience. If a new setting removes the trigger, concealment, comparison, or consequence, it has changed the mechanism. Rework that condition rather than claiming the same effect survived.

Adapt expression to the new performer and style. A realistic animal, a cartoon creature, and a human need not use identical facial motions. Preserve the expressive role through channels the design can support. Do not infer a real subject's mental state from a coded movement.

## Hand off the path, not just the result

When only a boundary state matters, endpoint images may be useful. When the route to that state is the performance, protect the intermediate phases: the look before the move, the failed correction, the other character's response, or the effort to recover composure.

Suggest action references, intermediate pose design, or alternative coverage only within the actual backend's capabilities. An analytical crop is not automatically the right generation input. Reference readability, effective input window, identity preservation, and motion preservation require separate checks.

Deliver the cards, allowed changes, evidence limits, and any requested reconstruction options. Do not relabel a missing reaction as an environment shot or substitute explanatory text for an absent visual highlight. A sound effect can support a visible event; it cannot prove the event happened.
