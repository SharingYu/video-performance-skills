# Original Visual Highlights

Build the highlight from a character's way of pursuing a goal, then decide how to show it. A hook establishes a worthwhile expectation; later behavior or consequences must develop or answer it.

## Develop mechanisms, not decorative variants

Identify the immediate goal, the usual strategy, the pressure that changes it, and any contrast with a partner. Distinguish what the audience knows from what each character knows.

When the user wants concept development, compare a small set of genuinely different mechanisms unless the requested count or scope says otherwise. A local repair or selected idea does not need several replacement stories. Changing food, occupation, costume, or location alone is not a different mechanism.

Useful possibilities include:

- A performer tries to preserve dignity while an observable result undermines it.
- Great effort or precision is applied to the wrong target or an unnecessarily elaborate solution.
- Two personalities pursue the same goal differently, making the comparison and reactions the highlight.
- The audience recognizes a risk before the character, creating anticipation of the consequence.
- A previously established condition changes the meaning of apparent success.

Choose what fits. Do not force every event to use a reversal, a repeated pattern, embarrassment, or a final reaction shot. Warmth, relief, recognition, and satisfaction can also be the payoff.

## Make the idea visible

For each candidate, state the expectation, the choice that changes it, the understandable consequence, and the expressive carrier: a posture, gaze shift, interrupted task, physical result, reaction contrast, or composition. Specify how attention reaches that carrier.

If the description only says "funny," "cute," or "more expressive," develop a behavior and its cause. Random new objects or unexplained rule changes are surprising without necessarily being satisfying. Language-led formats may use necessary dialogue, but explanatory captions should not replace the visual event being promised.

## Diagnose and choose

Use only the questions that expose the bottleneck:

- **Too obvious:** Does the audience receive the answer before there is anything worth waiting for?
- **Too obscure:** Which contextual cue is missing? Add a usable cue rather than explaining the whole joke.
- **Over-explained:** What can be removed while leaving the audience able to make the connection?
- **Out of character:** Would this performer choose that tactic, or is the behavior only serving the author's desired result?
- **Bloated setup:** Which passage neither builds expectation, changes a choice, raises a consequence, nor makes a reaction readable?

Where independent review is available and useful, provide the brief and candidates without a preferred answer. Compare the observations, then make a reasoned choice. Otherwise label self-review honestly. Do not turn repeated user confirmation into the evaluation process.

Deliver the relevant comparison, the chosen mechanism, and its [event card](event-card.md). Keep designed intentions separate from audience evidence. Structural reasoning cannot predict laughter or reach; retain actual feedback when available without treating one response as a general success rate.
